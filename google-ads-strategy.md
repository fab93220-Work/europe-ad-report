### S01 · Brand Search — £30/day
- Exact + phrase on "lincs rads direct", "lincsradsdirect", misspellings.
- Bid: Target Impression Share (absolute top, 95%). Cheapest, highest-ROAS clicks in the account — protect them from competitors and from PMax cannibalisation.
- **Add your brand terms to the brand exclusion list on every PMax campaign** so PMax can't claim brand conversions and flatter its own ROAS.

### S02 · Column Radiators — £80/day (hero)
Ad groups (exact + phrase, one theme each):
1. Core — column radiators, column radiator
2. Vertical — vertical column radiators, tall column radiator
3. Horizontal — horizontal column radiators, 2/3/4 column radiator
4. Colours — anthracite / white / black / grey / raw metal column radiator
5. Electric — electric column radiator
- RSAs: pin category headline pos. 1 ("Column Radiators | Free UK Delivery"), include price-point, guarantee, delivery-time headlines. Sitelinks to vertical/horizontal/colour subcategories; price extensions.
- Bid: start Maximise Conversions → switch to tROAS after ~30 conversions in the campaign.

### S03 · Cast Iron Radiators — £40/day
Ad groups: core / traditional–victorian / by style (with-feet, school-style) / assembled vs flat-pack. Highest AOV in the catalogue justifies the ~£1.85+ CPCs. Negative: "reclaimed", "second hand", "restoration" (unless you sell those).

### S04 · Electric Radiators — £50/day
Ad groups: electric radiators core / electric wall-mounted / electric vertical / electric towel rail (cross-link with S06 via negatives so queries route once). Biggest untapped volume pool (21k/month).

### S05 · Designer / Vertical / Aluminium — £40/day
Ad groups: designer radiators / vertical radiators / flat panel / mirror / aluminium. "Vertical radiators" (13k/month at ~£1) is the workhorse here.

### S06 · Towel Rails — £30/day
Ad groups: heated towel rail / electric towel rail / colours (black, brass, chrome) / bathroom radiators. Cheap CPCs (£0.30–0.50) mean this small budget still buys 60–100 clicks/day.

**Search-wide hygiene:**
- Shared negative list applied to all non-brand Search: `car radiator`, `radiator repair`, `bleed`, `leak`, `not working`, `bmw|ford|vauxhall...`, `cover`, `key`, `cheap` (optional), `b&q`, `screwfix`, `wickes` and other retailer brands you don't want to pay comparison-shopper CPCs on.
- Exclude pure-generic "radiators" from exact targeting — let PMax/Shopping catch it at feed-level CPCs instead.

### P01 · PMax — Column + Cast Iron — £220/day (hero)
Your traffic light: highest-margin categories get the deepest budget and their own ROAS target.
- Listing groups: column + cast iron products only (filter by product_type or custom_label_0 = "hero").
- Asset groups (one per audience/theme, each with its own signal):
  1. Column — lifestyle imagery, search-theme signals: column radiator terms above
  2. Cast iron — period-home imagery, cast iron search themes
  3. Colour trend — anthracite/black/raw metal
- tROAS: set ~20% below your blended target initially (e.g. target 600% → launch at 480%), tighten every 2 weeks.
- Feed: titles front-loaded with `[Type] Radiator [Orientation] [Colour] [Size mm]` — e.g. "Column Radiator Vertical Anthracite 1800×290mm 2 Column". Size and colour in titles is the single biggest Shopping CTR lever in this vertical.

### P02 · PMax — Electric + Aluminium — £90/day
Separate because the buyer (retrofit/no-plumbing, eco-conscious) and price point differ. Asset groups: electric radiators / electric towel rails / aluminium.

### P03 · PMax — Towel Rails + Rest of catalogue — £70/day
Everything else, including accessories (valves — 15k searches/month at £0.19 makes a nice basket-builder). Lower tROAS tolerance is fine here; it's the discovery layer.

### SH01 · Standard Shopping — Catch-all — £30/day
- **Note:** PMax takes priority over Standard Shopping for the same products, so this only serves where PMax doesn't. Its job is (a) query-level visibility you can't get in PMax — mine its search-terms report weekly for new Search keywords and PMax search themes; (b) a safety net if a PMax campaign is paused/limited.
- Low-priority campaign, Manual CPC or tROAS, all products.
- If after 60 days it spends nothing meaningful, fold the £30 into P01.

### D01 · Demand Gen — £20/day
Remarketing to site visitors + Customer Match lookalikes. Radiators are a considered purchase (research on mobile, buy on desktop days later) — this keeps you in the journey. Creative: room-scene imagery by category.

## 4. Prerequisites before scaling spend

1. **Conversion tracking:** purchase with cart-value, Enhanced Conversions enabled, Consent Mode v2 (mandatory UK/EEA). Import as primary conversion; add-to-basket as secondary (observation only).
2. **Merchant Centre:** GTIN/MPN coverage, `product_type` taxonomy mirroring site categories, `custom_label_0` = margin band (hero / mid / low) to drive the PMax split, promotions feed for offers, product ratings if you have reviews.
3. **New customer acquisition goal** in PMax with a realistic LTV uplift value.
4. **Seasonality:** radiator demand peaks Sep–Feb. Plan to hold ~£700/day through summer at efficiency targets, then push to £900–1,000/day from September when CVR climbs.

## 5. Ramp plan

- **Weeks 1–2:** Launch Search (Max Conversions) + P01 only. Let conversion data accumulate. Watch search terms daily; build negatives.
- **Weeks 3–4:** Launch P02, P03, SH01. Move Search campaigns with 30+ conversions to tROAS.
- **Month 2:** Launch D01. Set tROAS on PMax. Rebalance: shift budget toward whichever of S02/P01 shows the better marginal ROAS — they'll compete for the same auctions and one will win.
- **Ongoing weekly:** search-terms mining (SH01 + Search → new keywords/negatives), PMax placement exclusions, feed title tests on top-50 SKUs.

## 6. KPIs to judge it by

- Blended MER (revenue / total ad spend) as the north star, not in-platform ROAS alone (PMax over-credits itself, especially near brand).
- Per-campaign: tROAS attainment, impression share lost to budget (raise winners), conversion lag (expect 3–7 days in this vertical — judge weeks, not days).
- Brand vs non-brand split reported separately every week.
